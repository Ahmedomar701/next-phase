15:T1551,<h2 id="commoditize-everything-in-healthcare">Commoditize everything in healthcare</h2>
<p>The basics are toast. Meeting notes? Scribe bots? Virtual receptionists? All headed to <code>$0</code>. They’ll be bundled into every SaaS suite, every EHR, every cloud console. Palantir, Google, Epic — they’ll ship “good enough” versions at scale, because scale is their religion.</p>
<p>But here’s the catch: good enough still costs them <code>200%</code> margins to protect those <code>$300M</code> VP comp lines. And good enough costs health systems billions of dollars in inefficiencies. They can’t afford to be <em>great</em>. They can only afford to be <em>good enough</em>.</p>
<p>That’s the opportunity.</p>
<p>Startups don’t win by competing on the commodity layer. We win by turning the <code>1%</code> edge into a <code>$100B</code> moat.</p>
<p>The average hospital uses <code>50–500</code> software tools. EHRs are dumb legacy databases whose UX and firewalls have caused fatal errors and human deaths; most of them are working very hard to justify their existence before they become obsolete. In healthcare, <code>1%</code> better isn’t a nice-to-have. It’s the difference between catching a suicide risk in a voice tremor and missing it — between flagging a lithium toxicity spike 48 hours early and reacting when the patient is in the ER.</p>
<p>Example: eClinicalWorks launched its own AI solutions; instead of focusing on ways to save physicians’ time, it takes <code>17</code> clicks to launch any AI feature.</p>
<p>Zoom out:</p>
<figure class="figure">
<pre class="figure-body figure-frame">                    commodity                         elite
people              $40K / year                       $1B  (Zuck's FB offers)
AI agents           voice agent that can              Sully receptionist:
                    handle patient calls, $0          schedules, defers &gt;50% of
                                                      humans, detects, acts,
                                                      wearable drift alerts
                                                      → $1B+ / year in outcomes</pre>
<figcaption><span class="figure-label">Fig. 1</span> The same delta, twice.</figcaption>
</figure>
<p><code>80%</code> of clinical work is <em>complex, time-sensitive, and multi-modal</em>. That’s not commoditizable with one <code>7B</code> model and a prompt. It needs:</p>
<ul>
<li><code>20</code> API calls to <code>10</code> different systems, excluding the EHR</li>
<li><code>10–20</code> different open-source LLM calls</li>
<li><code>3</code> wearables</li>
<li><code>1</code> EHR</li>
<li><code>1</code> genomic feed</li>
<li>a real-time QA loop</li>
</ul>
<p>Large players <em>can’t</em> move this fast. Their org charts won’t let them.</p>
<h2 id="the-premium-people-are-willing-to-pay-for">The premium people are willing to pay for</h2>
<p>Ask a psychiatrist:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Would you pay <code>$500</code>/month for an AI that texts your patient at 2am, detects flat affect, and books a crisis visit before you even wake up?</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Ask a daughter:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Would you pay <code>$99</code>/month to know Dad’s Apple Watch + voice + med adherence predicts a fall risk 6 months out?</p>
</blockquote>
<p>The answer isn’t “maybe.” It’s “shut up and take my card.”</p>
<p>The basic features of agents will be commoditized, and we will be the first to make them free — but we’re building the state-of-the-art systems that can autonomously track the holistic picture of your health, in commune with human doctors, to provide the absolute best preventative and high-quality care.</p>
<p>These experiences don’t exist at scale today. When they do, the TAM isn’t “clinics.” It’s every single <em>family</em>.</p>
<h2 id="sullys-play">Sully’s play</h2>
<p>We’re not building another agent. We’re building the autonomous OS — the orchestrator.</p>
<ul>
<li>Plug in Oura, Apple, Epic, Cerner, Groq, Anthropic, your custom RAG.</li>
<li>Route every patient interaction to the <em>right</em> model at the <em>right</em> latency and cost.</li>
<li>Run evals on <code>100%</code> of outputs (HIPAA-compliant, monitored by humans for safety).</li>
<li>Predict <code>6–8–20</code> months out: “Mrs. Chen’s lithium levels + sleep fragmentation → <code>87%</code> renal event risk by Q3 2026.”</li>
</ul>
<p>We’ll never do everything. But we’ll integrate <em>enough</em> to ship <strong>predictive care loops</strong> that no EHR dares to touch.</p>
<h2 id="the-autonomous-operating-system">The autonomous operating system</h2>
<p>Agents are coming. You’ll need a PhD agent to tune one for rare-disease diagnostics. You’ll need a GM agent to run <code>47</code> of them in parallel. And we will need a better operating system that can intelligently store, manage, and reason over all these concurrent operations.</p>
<p>At Sully, we’re building that AutonomousOS, and this isn’t just a nice vision on the wall; we’ve already seen it from customers.</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Feedback from a Sully.ai customer.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Commoditized AI will handle <code>20%</code> of the edge cases. We will own the <code>80%</code> where <em>lives compound</em>.</p>
<p>The race isn’t to build the model, the agent, or a use-case. It’s to build the system that <em>autonomously</em> operates systems.</p>
