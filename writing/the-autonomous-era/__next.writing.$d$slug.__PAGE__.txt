2:T11b5,<p>We’ve been talking about this for quite a while — we keep saying “autonomous, autonomous, autonomous” — but what do we mean by autonomous, and how does it even work?</p>
<p>The best thing that happened over the past three years was the launch of <strong>ClawdBot (OpenClaw)</strong>. Not because it’s a nicer chatbot, or a more intelligent one, but because it worked <strong>autonomously</strong> — stitching over fifty tools, libraries, and platforms into one system. It made it feel magical.</p>
<p>The ClawdBot can go and read my Twitter, save my tone of voice into a simple markdown file, and schedule a post autonomously. It doesn’t stop there — it can do this all over again, every time, every day.</p>
<blockquote>
<p>This is what we mean by autonomous: an agent that sits on the doctor’s EHR, keeps a pulse and checks every few hours for any changes, then autonomously takes the next best action, the next best decision, without any human interaction.</p>
</blockquote>
<h2 id="the-hard-truth">The hard truth</h2>
<p>Before talking about how we have to move to building autonomous agents as fast as we can, I want to acknowledge the <strong>top three challenges</strong> in the company today:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Integration speed and quality</strong> — blocking revenue growth.</li>
<li><strong>Audio capture stability</strong> — inherited tech debt in Copilot.</li>
<li><strong>Database and platform issues</strong> — the scalability ceiling in Firebase.</li>
</ol>
<p>We’re making progress. But for the next <code>4–6</code> weeks, we’re taking this seriously. One hundred percent.</p>
<h2 id="revolution-meaning-enterprise-ready">Revolution, meaning enterprise-ready</h2>
<p>In order for us to move to the next phase, we’re going to hyper-focus for the next four to six weeks on integration, and on moving the platform from where it is to where it has to be.</p>
<figure class="figure">
<pre class="figure-body figure-frame">                availability    downtime / year
today                99.95%     ≈ 4 h 23 min
target             99.9999%     ≈ 32 s</pre>
<figcaption><span class="figure-label">Fig. 1</span> Reliability target, expressed as permitted annual downtime.</figcaption>
</figure>
<p>That is a <code>500×</code> reduction in tolerated failure.<sup class="footnote-ref"><a href="#fn1" id="fnref1">[1]</a></sup> This will be our <strong>highest priority in the company.</strong></p>
<p>In the next <code>6–8</code> weeks, we need to push as hard as we can — not just to look like a larger enterprise company such as Athena or Cerner, <code>$20</code> billion of technology that still breaks on clinicians every month, but to build a <strong>revolutionary platform that clinicians can rely on completely.</strong></p>
<p>Yes, I mean it. <strong>One hundred percent uptime and stability.</strong></p>
<p>Building an enterprise is very hard. Not because you need to get things right, but because you need multiple backups for everything — and on top of all that, you need the infrastructure to support it.</p>
<h2 id="the-autonomous-position">The autonomous position</h2>
<blockquote>
<p>“If you guys can do this for healthcare, this can truly disrupt the entire healthcare industry.”</p>
<p><em>— Chief of Staff, after watching our intelligence layer run</em></p>
</blockquote>
<p>We’re extremely lucky to be in this position today as a healthcare AI player. We’re in the <strong>best position</strong> because we don’t have the huge number of customers that would make us too slow to move — and we’re not too small either. We have customers who will literally try anything we tell them.</p>
<p>This is the best time to build a <code>$20</code> billion company: the ClawdBot for healthcare.</p>
<p>Our focus over the next few weeks will be on defining the <strong>core functions that all our agents perform</strong>, and on getting the team aligned on how agents safely collaborate and work autonomously for our customers.</p>
<p>It’s not going to be easy, but it’ll be <strong>hella fun.</strong></p>
